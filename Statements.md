# Project Statement & Scope

## 1. Problem Statement
Quantifying complex, multifaceted geopolitical crises into concrete, objective metrics is traditionally difficult due to the qualitative nature of conflict, international friction, and market pressure. Without an analytical baseline, assessing how distinct factors—such as sudden resource scarcity or inflation spikes—interact with regional border friction remains highly subjective. This project addresses this problem by converting qualitative conditions into normalized numerical inputs and processing them through fixed geometric equations, eliminating qualitative ambiguity and outputting a single, reproducible risk index.

## 2. Scope of the Project
The application computes predictive risk modeling across three critical indicators: domestic macroeconomic inflation, territorial border friction, and strategic global resource availability. It evaluates current variables relative to an embedded historical registry of 13 major crises spanning 1956 to 2024. The model provides clear mathematical scoring, algorithmic classification, and proximity comparisons within an educational, terminal-based framework.

## 3. Target Users
* **Geopolitical Researchers & Analysts:** Looking for a baseline conceptual framework to simulate indicator interactions.
* **Academic Institutions & Students:** Exploring how programming and mathematical vectors can model real-world risks.
* **Risk Management Professionals:** Studying relationships between economic pressure and political instability.

## 4. High-Level Features
* **Multi-Parameter Ingestion Engine:** Validates and normalizes qualitative indicators on a strict 1.0 to 10.0 scale.
* **Weighted Spatial Proximity Analysis:** Uses a weighted Euclidean distance matrix to compare current states with a historical case database.
* **Similarity-Weighted Risk Compositing:** Calculates historical risk by weighting past outcomes based on their mathematical relevance to the present.
* **Algorithmic Threat Classification System:** Groups final combined scores into three distinct alert levels (Low, Moderate, High Modeled Threat) for swift analysis.
